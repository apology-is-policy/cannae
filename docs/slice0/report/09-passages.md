## Both engines on the same bench

The bench runs a crowd through a gate and a maniple back through an interval: the discs at a
radius of 300 mm with the other parameters at their placeholders and the crush parameters at
their priors' centres, the field with every prior at its centre. Js is persons per metre of width per second, counted from the tenth
man to the ninetieth.

| | Measured | Field | Discs (300 mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking through a 3 m gate, Js | about 1.9 (1.6-2.3) | 1.81 (1.17 before calibration) | 2.91 |
| The same, steered across the whole mouth | | 1.60 | 2.01 |
| Running and shoving through a 3 m gate, Js | about 4.0, at 0.7 m doors | 5.70 | 6.27 |
| A maniple through a 3 m interval: nine in ten through | no data | 26.8 s | 24.1 s |
| The same, with the enemy following | no data | 26.8 s; 1 or 2 of 120 still not through | 33.1 s |
| Seyfried's 0.8-1.2 m corridor | 1.6-2.3 | cannot run: narrower than a cell | 1.7-2.4 |

- **A maniple passes an interval in under a minute** on both engines, at every width from 3
  to 12 m (16.6 to 33.1 s on the discs, 17.1 to 26.8 s on the field). A relief by intervals
  fits inside a lull of a few minutes.
- **On the discs, faster is slower at the narrowest interval only:** at 3 m the enemy's shove
  jams the maniple against the line, the longest wait between two men rises from 0.5 to
  6.9 s, and nine in ten take 33 s instead of 24. That is the clogging measured at doors, and
  it comes out of the physics with no rule for it. The pursuit does not slow the field's
  maniple; the men it leaves behind were caught by the pursuit, not by a jam.
- **Running and shoving, both engines run high** where data exist only for narrow doors.

## How each was calibrated on measured flows

- **The discs:** the radius was fitted to Seyfried's corridor and Liao's wide bottlenecks. Walking
  through the corridor fits best at 300 mm, and the prior was narrowed to 280-300 mm.
  Steered across the whole mouth, discs of 300 mm fit both experiments, Liao's wide
  bottlenecks at 2.06 to 2.31, 10 to 20 % above the 1.9 measured. On the bench's first
  steering, each man making for the nearest point of the mouth, they run 30 to 50 % high:
  most of that excess was the bench's steering, not the discs.
- **The field:** Varro made its walking curve's capacity a drawn parameter, U[1.6, 2.3]
  persons per metre per second, on the bottleneck measurements and on no pattern, and checked
  that the flow scales with it at every width from 2.4 to 5 m on Liao's geometry.
- **Neither steering was measured.** People in a waiting area do spread across a bottleneck,
  so steering across the whole mouth is the nearer. In the scored runs steering comes from the
  man model.

## The field's flow before its calibration

Before it, the field walked 1.17 persons per metre per second through a 3 m gate, **about 40 %
low;** at its calibrated centre it walks 1.81. The two engines had missed the walking flow in
opposite directions: the field passed the capacity of a walking curve fitted to people in the
open, and the discs keep full speed until they touch.

## The one difference in the press

**On the pairwise engine a loose man who presses shoves a formed man; on the field a man in
his place is moved by his tie alone.** The interval passage shows it. The bench has no frames,
so the line beside the interval is made of loose men who stand, and at 3 m the discs pushed
them up to 592 mm from their slots, the field's `Line` contact 101 mm. In a battle those men
are in their places, and the field does not move them at all. So in a scored run a man
squeezing past a formed line shoves it on the pairwise engine and not on the field.

## The limit that stands

**People push through a 0.7 m door about 40 % faster than discs.** The measured 4.0 needs
discs of about 240 mm, which walk through an open bottleneck nearly twice too fast. People
pushing through a gap turn their shoulders, 0.3 m from front to back and 0.5 m across, and a
disc is as long as it is wide, so in single file it caps a 0.7 m door at about 1.5 to 2
persons a second against the 2.8 measured. Military passages are wider, and there shoulders
matter less. Bodies that turn are possible remedies, and none is built.

**Who was measured.** The people in these experiments carried no shields. A man with a scutum
is wider than a man in a shirt, so both engines' calibrations may run high for armed men.
Neither has data for that, and neither guesses a correction.

## What it means

- **At military widths both engines pass men at about the measured rates** once men use the
  whole mouth, the field by calibration and the discs by their radius.
- **The discs still lack a speed that falls as the crowd thickens,** which costs 10 to 20 %
  through these gaps. In the scored runs that could act only in a jammed relief, and section
  5 found one jammed relief at each of the pairwise engine's rungs 0 and 1 and none at rungs
  2 and 3.
- **The two engines differ in the press,** and the interval shows how.
