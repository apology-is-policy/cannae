# Review of the Cannae vision and the command-AI proposal

I read all nine vision docs. The thesis is right and most of the engineering spine holds; I think a source-faithful A1 is reachable. As specified, though, the project could pass its own tests without demonstrating anything, for three reasons. A1 encodes a textbook reading that the sources contradict. The psyche has more overlapping mechanisms than the evidence can separate. Plan scripts and per-battle ratings can carry the outcome. The roadmap also meets its real risks last. Quotations were checked online against Polybius, Livy and Caesar unless marked "from memory".

## The most important points

### 1. A1 demands an elastic centre that the sources do not describe and the psyche model rules out

**Issue.** The README (line 63) and 03 §5 require a crescent that "bows back … but does not rupture". Polybius 3.115 says the Celts and Spaniards "gave way and fell back, breaking up the crescent", and that the maniples "easily penetrated the enemy's front". Livy 22.47 has the Romans carried through "pavore fugientium agmen", a column of men fleeing in panic, until "nullo resistente" they reached the Africans. About 4,000 Gauls died, roughly a fifth of those present. The centre was driven in, and the trap closed anyway. Goldsworthy's reconstruction, as I recall it, also has the centre finally collapse after holding long enough.

**Why it matters.** 04 §5–6 and 03 §5 say fierce, undrilled troops are bistable and snap, and that only well-drilled units can retire under pressure without routing. The Gauls are profiled as "weak drill". So the model as designed predicts outcome (c) at Cannae. Reaching P(a) ≥ 0.6 would then need Hannibal's morale field turned up for this one scenario, which is a fudge on the flagship test.

**Instead.** Define class (a) by what survives every reading: the Roman mass funnels into the pocket and loses its order; the Libyans strike both flanks; cavalry closes the rear; no formed Roman body gets out. Let the centre be driven in or locally routed. Define (c) as a formed body of several thousand exiting, as at the Trebia. Report "centre holds" and "centre breaks but the trap closes" as sub-classes: that is a real historical question the simulator can address.

**Confidence.** High on the sources. Medium-high on the design consequence.

### 2. The psyche equations do not do what the prose around them says

**Issue (04 §5–6).**
- Resolve R appears in no equation. Fear F and fury Φ enter only b, so they are interchangeable: fear does not push toward flight, and fury does not push toward attack.
- Raising b delays the break. The fight state survives until a < −2(b/3)^1.5, so the "brittle" high-arousal troops are the model's most stubborn. Low-b drilled troops start backing away as soon as a < 0. Roman staying power then rests entirely on order_bias, which enters both a and arbitration.
- Hysteresis lasts only while b is high. b decays away from combat, so a routed man out of contact becomes monostable and x follows a back up. Routs heal themselves.
- After the fold, x lands at −2√(b/3). A three-pace recoil and a rout differ only in magnitude, and neighbours couple to both. A recoil can seed a cascade, and an ordered withdrawal reads as flight to the unit's own men.
- Behaviour at the contact line is set by the closed loop from x to movement to distance to threat to a. That loop is never analysed. Depending on b near the enemy it gives a stable standoff, a surge-and-recoil oscillation, or lock-in until rout. S2 and M15 depend on which.

**Why it matters.** This is the declared heart of the project. Lulls, rout and rally all hang on these terms.

**Instead.** Write it as what it structurally is: an excitable system, with a fast approach–avoid variable and slow recovery variables (W′, R), like the FitzHugh–Nagumo model of a nerve cell. That structure is what produces pulses separated by refractory lulls. Analyse it in a one-dimensional toy of two short lines before any engine exists; it takes days. Add an explicit time constant and a noise term. Let contagion key on observable acts (a back turned, a shield dropped), never on x. Make a man's reading of comrades stepping back depend on the unit's current order. Keep integrator state in f32 or 16-bit fixed point: u8 quantisation stalls slow dynamics, and memory is not the constraint.

**Confidence.** High on the algebra. Medium on which repair is best; the toy decides.

### 3. Four mechanisms produce the same signature, and the evidence cannot separate them

**Issue.** Sudden collapse with hysteresis arises from four places:
- the individual cusp;
- neighbour coupling, since a coupled population is collectively bistable even when each man responds smoothly;
- the change in what a man perceives once he turns his back;
- slow R.

"Snap or crumble" has two knobs that predict opposite things. b(drill) says veterans degrade gracefully and warbands snap. The random-field Ising result says a narrow threshold spread (veterans) snaps and a wide one (a warband of heroes and followers) crumbles. The Gallic pattern is also not emergent: it is assumed through b(drill). The ancient explanation is endurance, which W′ without rotation already gives. I count roughly 50–100 free psyche and combat parameters before troop profiles, against 33 battles that each give about one bit and a disputed casualty figure.

**Why it matters.** At that ratio any outcome can be fitted, so a fit shows nothing.

**Instead.**
- Apply pattern-oriented modelling to design as well as validation: a mechanism enters only when a named pattern cannot be reproduced without it.
- In the toy, compare three individual kernels (cusp, two-threshold switch, smooth response). Keep the simplest that reproduces rear-first collapse, S1 and S2.
- Hold b equal across cultures unless evidence forces otherwise.
- Fix most parameters from independent evidence (physiology, load-carriage studies, reconstruction trials). Run global sensitivity analysis to find the ten or so that matter. Calibrate those by history matching, which rules out implausible regions instead of finding one best fit. Report the share of the surviving region in which each test passes.
- Formations are small, thin systems, so the snap-or-crumble transition will be blurred. M16 should compare avalanche-size distributions.

W′ is the best-grounded part. One to three minutes to exhaustion and published recovery constants of roughly 5–10 minutes (Skiba) give believable clash and lull lengths. Cost is not a concern: local appraisal can reuse the collision pass's neighbour scan.

**Confidence.** High.

### 4. Cannae is not a holdout, and much of 07 is verification

**Issue.**
- The designers know the answer, and the mechanics list was built from Cannae.
- Every scenario carries free inputs of its own: quality ratings, commander presets, the plan. Adjusting those on a holdout battle is fitting.
- S6, M10, M13 and others test rules that 04 writes in directly (horses refuse, veterans pace themselves, only trained troops can feign). Those are unit tests.
- The corpus is famous battles, which over-samples upsets.
- P(a) between 0.6 and 0.95 is a design target. History gives one draw.
- Arausio, Vercellae and the Great Plains have almost no usable narrative.

**Why it matters.** This is where overfitting enters, and none of it shows up in a parameter count.

**Instead.**
- Call Cannae the development scenario. Seal three to five other battles: dossiers written and hash-committed before the engine can run them, then one pre-registered run.
- Derive ratings and personalities from a fixed rubric, written without sight of results.
- Score sealed battles by the log-likelihood of the historical outcome, against simple baselines.
- Prefer contrasts and dose-response curves to point probabilities: Cannae, Dertosa and the Trebia under one parameter set; P(centre breaks) against centre quality; P(rear closed) against cavalry ratio. P(c) ≥ 0.03 cannot be established with 100 seeds (a 5 % chance of seeing none).
- Sample input uncertainty continuously instead of three source variants.
- Fix a stopping rule and a mapping to dead, wounded and captured. The source totals include the pursuit and the camps.

**Confidence.** High.

### 5. The plan DSL needs a script budget

**Issue.** Polybius records no orders for Cannae. A "Hannibal plan" in the DSL is the outcome reverse-engineered into the input. Polybius 3.116 credits Hasdrubal's own judgment for leaving the pursuit to the Numidians and riding to support the Africans. If that is a trigger, A1 is a puppet show.

**Why it matters.** The more of the plan is authored, the less a pass means.

**Instead.** Run A1 at three authoring tiers and report all three. Tier 0 is deployment plus generic doctrine. Tier 1 adds purposes. Tier 2 adds full triggers. The claim worth making is Tier 0 or 1, and I think it is within reach: the Libyans need only "attack an exposed flank within reach", and the cavalry needs "after winning the wing, rally and choose the most valuable target", weighted by discipline. Authoring rule: nothing a commander could not have said aloud beforehand, and triggers only over what the assignee can perceive, in coarse terms (few, half, most).

**Confidence.** High on the risk. Medium that Tier 0 passes.

### 6. A2 is not yet a test

**Issue.** Beating a Lanchester predictor is trivial, and naming a decisive event after the fact is always possible.

**Why it matters.** As written, added noise passes A2.

**Instead.**
- Decompose variance by RNG stream. The counter-based RNG lets you re-randomise one stream class (trait draws, combat dice, perception noise, command and messengers) with the others frozen. The resulting sensitivity indices say how much of the outcome was fixed by hidden traits at setup, how much is dice, and how much is command friction. That gives "not noise" a number.
- Use a strong predictor. Train a model on thousands of small simulated battles from pre-battle observables only. A2 is its residual uncertainty on balanced scenarios, plus a predictability-over-time curve that should rise in steps at identifiable events.
- Define "balanced" with a scenario generator. Historical battles were the ones both commanders accepted, which is a selection effect sandbox setups lack.

**Confidence.** Medium-high.

### 7. Performance: plan for 3–7× real time at Cannae, on a fanless 8 GB Air

**Issue.** `sysctl` reports this machine as Mac14,2: an M2 MacBook Air with 8 GB. The arithmetic in 06 §8 is correct, and 17 G cycles/s is near the all-core ceiling. Three inputs are optimistic.
- Active fraction. "10–25 % near contact" fails in the phase A1 cares about. In the encirclement nearly every man is under pressure, for hours.
- Unit cost. 600–1,000 cycles is roughly what tuned particle codes spend on collision alone, and a double buffer in which each agent writes only itself visits every pair twice. Per-soldier cone sampling of L2 and L3 with occlusion costs thousands of cycles per think. I would budget 1,200–2,500 until measured.
- Scaling and heat. Serial phases, plus typically 15–25 % sustained throttling without a fan.

**Why it matters.** That is 5–10 Cannae seeds a night, not 50–100. N of 100–500 across 33 battles, variants and modes is out by orders of magnitude. The live viewer is safe at 1–4×. Anything faster must be playback of recorded runs.

**Instead.** Measure sustained throughput in week one. Share long-range perception per file or century, since interior ranks see only backs. Calibrate at a few thousand agents, where runs take seconds. Rent a many-core machine for acceptance batches.

**Confidence.** Medium-high on direction, medium on magnitude.

### 8. The hard AI is the middle layer, and the roadmap reaches the risk last

**Issue.** Low command bandwidth is true of generals. It is not true of the thousand-plus centurion-level controllers deciding when to charge, relieve, face a flank or close a gap. That layer and the unit frame beneath it (a reference fitted to where the men actually are, slot reassignment, passing through intervals without jamming) get one bullet in Phase 3. I expect them to be the largest effort sink. The phases run physics, combat, psyche, command, so acceptance-critical risk surfaces in Phases 6–8. Phase 4's exit tests M4 and M15 need rout and fear, which arrive in Phase 5.

**Why it matters.** Nothing in Phases 1–5 can tell you whether A1 or A2 is reachable.

**Instead: vertical slices.**
- **Slice 0, two spikes (one to two weeks).** The behaviour toy from point 2: do a standoff, pulses, rear-first collapse and S1 appear over a non-trivial parameter region? A performance spike: 150,000 agents, collision, a dummy think with realistic memory traffic, sustained cycles per agent-tick, identical hash on 1, 4 and 8 threads.
- **Slice 1, maniple against warband, and the turned flank (500–5,000 agents).** The real deterministic core, unit frame, three drills, W′, psyche v1, a centurion state machine, one superior with a two-task plan and a messenger, the inspector, batch metrics. Micro-tests M2, M7, M15, M16. Sensitivity analysis happens here.
- **Slice 2, Little Cannae at 1:10.** Two cavalry types, rally and re-task, the scene graph, the plan representation, eight to ten plays, compression. Minutes per seed, so a hundred-plus overnight: the tier ladder, the Dertosa and Trebia contrasts, the A2 decomposition, and a scale study (1:20, 1:10, 1:5).
- Then scale up, and add breadth as the corpus demands. Start Cannae scenarios after the skirmishers have withdrawn.

Make the knowledge base demand-driven: source the six troop types on this path, then whatever sensitivity analysis flags. The vision's 17,600 words needed the thirteen corrections listed below. A citation-level KB written ahead of need will need more.

**Confidence.** High.

## Secondary notes

**Command AI and DSL**
- On Michal's worry: I disagree that only an LLM can adapt a plan. The corpus's famous improvisations (the tribune at Cynoscephalae, Nero at the Metaurus) are recognitions of a standard opportunity: an unengaged force and a reachable flank or rear. A play gated by initiative covers them.
- Scene graph: compute true bodies once per side, then each officer's visibility by angular occlusion with eye height and terrain. Do not cluster per officer. From 2.4 m on a plain he sees a front edge; depth and anything behind the first line are largely unknown (Caesar's fourth line). Late-bound role queries for the enemy and the org tree for friends is the right split. Use facing coherence to tell a mob from an orbis.
- One language for three users pulls three ways. Define a small core representation the AI can generate and repair. Authoring syntax and visual scripting compile to it.
- Decision model: Klein's recognition-primed decision making (match cues to a play, check that one play, accept or move on) fits officers better than utility-argmax. Defer the look-ahead simulation: it needs a body-level combat model and an enemy-response model, and its artefacts become the AI's habits.
- Build that body-level model anyway, as its own crate behind a world-view trait. It is the officer's mental model, a fast test bed for command AI, and a cheap rung for calibration.
- The Roman failure at Cannae is mostly soldier-level (the inward surge) and structural (no reserve). Roman officers should try to react and go unheard.
- LLM out of the loop: agreed. The strongest reason is missing from your list: an LLM has read about Cannae. Keep it away from corpus plans. "LLM proposes, simulator scores" yields a commander who has rehearsed against the true enemy plan; score against enemy plans sampled from his beliefs.
- Time model: agreed as proposed. Record player inputs in the replay, and name two player modes: experimenter (truth, instant) and role-player (beliefs, messengers).

**Implementation**
- Crates: the four are fine. Add the command and body-level crates above, and a metrics crate. The outcome classifiers define A1 and belong in tested Rust.
- Double buffering does not settle pairwise events. Add an intent pass and a gather or sorted-event resolve from day one. Split parallel work by fixed block size.
- 06 §9 contradicts itself: bit-exact re-simulation cannot start from u16-quantised snapshots. Keep sparse full-precision keyframes, plus a quantised stream for playback.
- GPU compute on simulation state breaks bit-exactness across machines, including the rented one. Drop that lever.
- Golden replays guard refactors only. Behaviour regression is statistical, paired on common seeds.
- Double-buffer only an observables lane (position, velocity, facing, posture), and let agents read nothing else about each other. Private state updates in place. The type system then enforces both determinism and the fog-of-war rule. L2's "mean x" must become observable aggregates.
- Keep the fat state in unit and file order, which is already spatially coherent, and sort a compact proxy each tick. A dense 2 m grid over 5 × 5 km is 6 million cells; hash or bound it.
- Think rate sets panic-wave speed. Make reaction time a model parameter and assign phase by hash. LOD thresholds are model parameters too; test on/off equivalence at small scale.
- Model rumours as diffusing fields with a fixed vocabulary.
- Pad per-thread accumulators to 128 bytes, this CPU's cache line. 8 GB is tight for rust-analyzer plus wgpu builds, so keep sim-only builds light.

**Scope**
- Cannae needs about ten mechanisms; the vision lists several times that. Defer until a slice needs them: per-zone bleeding, helmet-specific hearing, glycogen and hydration, shield drift, language barriers, omens, looting, elephants, chariots, horse archers, sarissa mechanics, weather, fortifications.

**Open questions**
- Modes: frontage, intervals and othismos gain are parameters; keep them. Build one relief mechanism (R2) with a seam for others. Drop the claim that the corpus discriminates R1–R4: the model's premise is Sabin's, so R1 loses by construction, and outcome fit cannot separate them. Feasibility results (does line passage jam, and when) are legitimate.
- KB or skeleton first: neither. The Slice 0 spikes first.
